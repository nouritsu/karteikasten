Karteikasten teaches English speakers the 2,000 most common German words, one card at a time, with spaced repetition. It works offline and looks like a terminal: text in the Iosevka font and boxes drawn with lines, in Catppuccin Mocha (dark) or Latte (light), or whichever your phone is in.

The words
* The 2,000 most frequent German words, ranked from film subtitles and news and web text.
* Each with its article in its gender's colour, IPA, syllables and a recording from Wikimedia Commons.
* English meanings, the forms that matter (plurals, cases, conjugations), and example sentences from Tatoeba with their translations.

Studying
* A card starts at Ready!; tap it and the word shuffles in while the score starts counting down.
* Hints help, at a cost in points: Audio, Forms, Sentence and Cognate.
* Answer Know it or Don't know. The score grades your answer for FSRS spaced repetition: words you know come back rarely, the ones you don't come back soon.
* Set your level, A1.1 to B1.2, and the words below it come as quick checks while new words start past it. When your checks go well, the day's summary suggests the next level.
* A reminder at the time you set, on days with cards due that you haven't studied yet.

Stats
* Over 30 days, 90 days, a year, all time or any range, set against the span before.
* History, score, recall, coverage by rank, a 12-week grid of days, your study rhythm, records and the words you keep forgetting.
* Tap or drag across a chart to read it; reorder the blocks or put some away.

Words
* Search by German word or English meaning; ae finds ä.
* Filter by status, type and gender; mark a word known, pause it or reset it.

Your data
* Export and import JSON backups, a file a day or one file kept up to date, and schedule regular exports that run with the app closed.
* Android's cloud backup is off unless you turn it on; a direct transfer to a new phone works either way.

Private
* No network permission, no account, no ads, no analytics.
